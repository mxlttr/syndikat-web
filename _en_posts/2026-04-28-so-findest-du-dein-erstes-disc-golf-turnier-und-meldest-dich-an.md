If you've never played a disc golf tournament, getting started in Germany can quickly seem unnecessarily complicated. Where do you even find tournaments? Which platform is the right one? Do you have to be a club member? And why does it all often look as if you first have to understand three other websites before you can even sign up?

That's exactly what this guide is for. It's aimed at complete beginners and at club players who have played a few rounds but are still completely lost when it comes to their first tournament. The aim isn't to cover every detail of the German tournament scene, but to show you a practical way to get started at all.

## The easiest way in: find a tournament first, then figure out the rest

For official tournaments in Germany, the first port of call is in many cases [turniere.discgolf.de](https://turniere.discgolf.de) (in German). That's where most people end up first when they look for their first tournament. The problem: the site is functional, but not exactly a model of an easy introduction.

That's why we built our [disc golf tournament map](/en/turniere/). It gives you a much better overview of which tournaments are coming up, where they take place and which options are currently relevant. You can also show tournaments from Disc Golf Metrix there.

> If you just want to see some tournaments first, our [tournament map](/en/turniere/) is the best first step.

If you want to stay closer to the action, [our Discord](https://community.syndikat.golf) is also worth joining. In our `#tournaments` channel, a bot posts in the morning when registration for a tournament opens that day. Especially for popular events, that's more practical than happening to check the official site at some point later.

## Which platforms really matter in Germany

As a beginner, you don't need to know every platform in detail straight away. It's enough to roughly understand what each site is for.

### turniere.discgolf.de

For many official tournaments in Germany, this is the most important place to go. If you want to sign up for classic tournaments, you'll hardly be able to avoid this platform.

### Disc Golf Metrix

Metrix also plays an important role and is supported on our [tournament map](/en/turniere/) as well. Some events, leagues or series run there instead of on other systems. For you as a player, this mainly means: not every tournament uses exactly the same platform.

### UDisc

Many people know UDisc mainly from leagues and casual play. Local leagues and relaxed formats in particular often run there. It can still be relevant for getting into tournaments, because it gives you a first feel for competition and scoring. If you want to see what that looks like in practice, you can find [our league on UDisc](https://udisc.com/leagues/syndication-weekly) there too.

## Roughly how registration works

If you boil the process down to the essentials, it usually looks like this:

1. find a tournament on a platform or via our [tournament map](/en/turniere/)
2. check whether registration is already open
3. check whether there are still spots available or whether there's already a waiting list
4. create an account on the platform in question
5. register
6. pay, and then hope that you actually get a spot

That sounds simple, but the first time it often feels less clear-cut. That's exactly why it makes sense not to click "register" blindly, but to read the tournament information properly first.

## Before you get confused: your profile on the platform must be complete

On `turniere.discgolf.de` in particular, a first registration sometimes fails not because the tournament is full, but because of trivial profile details. If you can log in but can't select a suitable division when registering, or only get an unclear error message, it's often down to your profile.

These points are particularly important:

- fill in your year of birth
- fill in your gender
- accept the privacy policy

Without these details, the system may not treat you the way you expect. It's not a particularly elegant introduction, but it's good to know before you start doubting yourself.

## Do I need a club membership for every tournament?

Not for every tournament across the board, but a membership can be relevant for some tournaments. That's exactly why you shouldn't guess, but always read the tournament announcement and information.

If you want to play more often anyway, a club membership can make sense. If that's something you're considering, you'll find the most important information about our club under [Become a member](/en/register/).

## Which tournaments are good for beginners?

Not every tournament is the ideal start. If you're looking for your first event, smaller and more manageable formats usually help more than huge events with strong fields.

In our view, good first steps for many beginners are:

- local leagues
- the Westfalen-Tour (WT)
- tournaments such as the Bonnsai Open

The reason is simple: there you're more likely to find a setting in which you can find your bearings, rather than ending up straight away in an environment that stresses you out more than it helps you.

## These points almost always confuse beginners

There are a few things that almost everyone gets briefly stuck on at their first tournament.

### Waiting lists

Just because you've registered doesn't automatically mean you're guaranteed a place in the field. Popular tournaments have waiting lists or allocate spots later on. That's exactly why it's worth keeping an eye on your status after registering.

### Payment and bank details

This also quickly causes confusion the first time: payment information isn't immediately visible for every tournament. Depending on how the tournament director set up the event, you might see the bank details right after registering, or only later, or only once you actually have a spot.

So if bank details don't appear straight away, that's not automatically an error.

### Different platforms

Part of the confusion simply comes from the fact that not everything runs through the same site. You don't need to understand this perfectly to get started. What matters more is: find the right tournament first, then go along with that tournament's particular system.

### Rules and tournament information

At the beginning, many people turn a blind eye to obvious beginner questions. Still, you shouldn't turn up unprepared. Especially before your first tournament, it's important to read the caddy book. It contains the relevant information on the schedule, special rules, course details, check-in and often organisational specifics too.

### Withdrawing and extra options

This is part of reality too: sometimes you sign up and later realise it doesn't work out after all. Then you should withdraw properly rather than simply not showing up. Extra options such as food, player packs or other tournament options can often be edited later in your own registration status for many events. The exact process depends on the platform, but the main thing is: after registering, it's worth taking a second look at your tournament status.

## What you should definitely do before the tournament

Once you have a spot, the more useful preparation actually begins.

### 1. Read the caddy book

This is the most important point. It explains how the tournament really works. Not in theory, but specifically for this event.

### 2. Don't expect too much of yourself

You can expect a lot from the tournament itself, because that's exactly what makes it appealing. New people, a different kind of excitement, a clear structure and usually just a really good day of disc golf. What you shouldn't expect: that under tournament pressure you'll immediately play as freely and consistently as in a relaxed after-work round.

Most people are nervous at their first tournament, make a few bad decisions or throw shots they would otherwise play more calmly. That's normal and not a sign that tournaments aren't for you.

### 3. Prepare your equipment sensibly

You don't need to own everything, but you shouldn't turn up completely unprepared. For concrete preparation, our post on the [tournament packing list](/en/blog/turnier-packliste/) will help you.

## Our club's perspective on your first tournament

When someone at the Syndikat wants to play their first tournament, we would essentially tell them three things:

- Ask if you're unsure.
- Just sign up once rather than thinking about it forever.
- Don't let your first result discourage you.

For many people, the biggest progress doesn't happen in relaxed rounds with friends, but at tournaments. There you learn how to throw under pressure, how to organise yourself and how to deal with mistakes when they cost more than just a private round. That's exactly why a first tournament can be incredibly valuable despite the nerves.

## Conclusion

Signing up for a disc golf tournament in Germany seems more complicated at first than it really needs to be. But once you've been through the process properly, it quickly becomes clear: you don't need to know everything about associations, platforms and special cases. You just need to know where to start.

The best first step is therefore: look for a tournament on our [tournament map](/en/turniere/), read the information on the platform in question, sign up and then gain confidence through the caddy book and sensible preparation.

If you have any questions, ask at our club or in the community. And if you want to play more often, take a look at [Become a member](/en/register/) or our [FAQ](/en/faq/).
