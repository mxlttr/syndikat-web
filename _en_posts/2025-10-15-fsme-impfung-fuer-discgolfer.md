## October & November – health months for disc golfers

The season is drawing to a close. Fewer tournaments, more rest – time to do something good for body and mind. As a disc golfer, you often spend time in woods and meadows where ticks are waiting for their next host. Now is the perfect time to check your TBE protection and plan your prevention.

## TBE: what is it?

TBE (tick-borne encephalitis, known in German as FSME) is a viral infection that can attack the brain and nervous system. It is transmitted by ticks, especially in risk areas such as Bavaria, Baden-Württemberg, Hesse or Thuringia. [Symptoms range from mild fever to severe neurological problems.](https://www.rki.de/DE/Aktuelles/Publikationen/RKI-Ratgeber/Ratgeber/Ratgeber_FSME.html?nn=16777040#doc16791202bodyText7) (in German)

## Lyme disease – not to be confused with TBE

Ticks can also transmit Lyme disease – a bacterial infection for which there is no vaccine. Only caution and early treatment help. Lyme disease can affect the joints, skin and nervous system and occurs everywhere in Germany.

A good example is [pro disc golfer Ricky Wysocki](https://www.lymedisease.org/back-from-lyme-disease-disc-golfing-champ-has-some-advice-for-you/):

* In 2019, he was bitten by a tick in Europe and developed Lyme disease.
* Weeks later, extreme fatigue, a drop in performance and a characteristic rash appeared.
* He had to miss several tournaments and began intensive treatment before he could play again.
* His experience shows: even pros are not immune – prevention and fast treatment are crucial.

**Takeaway:** You protect yourself against TBE by getting vaccinated, and against Lyme disease only through caution and checks. Take both seriously so that your hobby doesn't put your health at risk.

### Risk areas in Germany

* [High-risk federal states: Bavaria, Baden-Württemberg, parts of Hesse, Thuringia, Saxony and Rhineland-Palatinate.](https://www.rki.de/DE/Themen/Infektionskrankheiten/Infektionskrankheiten-A-Z/F/FSME/Karte.html) (in German)
* Even in areas that are not "official" risk areas: ticks lurk everywhere – in woods, parks and meadows.

## The TBE vaccination – how the protection works

**Basic schedule:**

1. Vaccination: day 0
2. Vaccination: 1–3 months later
3. Vaccination: 5–12 months after the second dose

**Rapid schedule** (for short-notice trips to risk areas):

1. Vaccination: day 0
2. Dose after 14 days
3. Dose 5–12 months later (after the trip)

**Booster:**

* Under 60: every 5 years
* 60 and over: every 3 years

The vaccination is safe, effective and highly recommended, especially for disc golfers who regularly spend time in risk areas. A small jab can save you weeks of downtime or worse.

## Tick protection tips for disc golfers

1. Clothing: wear long trousers, light colours and socks over your trousers.
2. Tick repellent: use repellents containing DEET or permethrin.
3. Check after your round: remove any tick immediately.
4. The combination counts: vaccination + protective measures = maximum protection.

## Quick FAQ

**Only a few hours outside = no risk?**
Wrong. Ticks can be anywhere, and brief encounters are enough.

**Vaccination is dangerous?**
Side effects are very rare; the TBE vaccination is considered safe.

**Can I still get vaccinated during the season?**
Yes. The rapid schedule provides protection even at short notice.

## Conclusion – take precautions so you can play longer

TBE vaccination + tick protection = worry-free disc golf.
Use October and November to plan your health. An appointment with your GP can make all the difference – for a relaxed and healthy season next year.
