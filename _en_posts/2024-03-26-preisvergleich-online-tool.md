Anyone buying disc golf discs for the first time quickly realises: it's not quite that simple. There are lots of shops online, local stores often have little choice, and used discs sound cheap at first but aren't automatically beginner-friendly. That's exactly why it's worth taking a moment before you buy to think about which route actually makes sense for you.

This post isn't about naming the one perfect shop. Instead, we look at where it makes sense to buy disc golf discs, which mistakes beginners in particular often make and when our [price comparison](/en/preisvergleich/) really helps.

## Buying online is usually the most sensible option

If you want to buy disc golf discs in Germany, in many cases there's little way around buying online. The selection in local shops is usually very small or non-existent. Especially if you don't happen to live near a well-stocked shop, you simply get a better overview online.

The biggest advantage: you can compare models, weights, plastics and availability directly. This is especially helpful if you already roughly know what you're looking for, or if you've picked up specific models from an article like our post on [beginner discs](/en/blog/die-besten-scheiben-fuer-discgolf-einsteiger/).

In our view, all the shops in our [price comparison](/en/preisvergleich/) are recommendable. The purpose of the tool is therefore not to separate good shops from bad ones, but to make it easier for you to compare price and availability.

## Buying used can be good, but isn't automatically easier for beginners

At first glance, used discs seem like the perfect way to start: a lower price, less risk, maybe even straight from the community. In practice, though, it's a bit more complicated.

The problem isn't buying used as such, but the selection. Used discs often include exactly those models that others weren't happy with themselves. And these discs aren't automatically suitable for beginners. If you can't yet judge what speed, stability, wear and plastic actually mean in play, you can easily end up buying something that was cheap but still doesn't suit you.

That doesn't mean you have to avoid used discs altogether. On the contrary: if someone from the club or your group passes on a suitable disc and briefly explains why it makes sense for you, that can be a very good way to go. It gets trickier when you buy used somewhere without any guidance and are basically deciding based on a picture and a price.

## Asking at the club or other players is worth it

For many beginners, the best middle ground isn't "online only" or "used only", but: talk to people first, then buy with a purpose. If you ask other players, you'll often get a sensible recommendation faster than from hours of browsing shops.

At the Syndikat, this is easy. If you have questions or want to hold some discs in your hand, just talk to people. Many are happy to show you what they throw, and sometimes there's even a chance to pick up or try out a suitable disc right away. Especially for your first purchase, this is often more helpful than any product description.

## The mistakes beginners make most often when buying

We see a few patterns again and again:

### Buying starter sets blindly

Starter sets sound practical, but aren't automatically well put together. Often they simply contain gear that sells well, but doesn't necessarily suit beginners.

### Buying drivers that are too fast

Fast drivers seem exciting, but rarely help at the start. If you don't yet have the technique for them, you often end up with less control rather than more distance.

### Expensive pro tour discs for your first purchase

Just because a disc looks good in a shop or on social media doesn't mean it's the best way to start. Especially at the beginning, a sensible, easy-to-control setup does more for you than the next signature disc.

### Buying too cheaply

Very cheap discs or low-quality plastic seem attractive at first, but often don't last long enough. Especially when the rim quickly picks up dings, the flight changes unnecessarily early.

### Buying used without guidance

Used can be good, but only if you know what you're actually buying. Without guidance, price alone isn't a good yardstick.
